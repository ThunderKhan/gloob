# THE CORE CHALLENGE — WHAT MUST BE BUILT

Every valid character portfolio must include **all six** of the following:

1. **Identity:** Introduce the character's appearance, nature, and personality.
2. **Profession/work:** Showcase at least **three** things the character has made or worked on; these can be fictional.
3. **Story:** Reveal an origin or journey through scrolling, scenes, animation, or similarly visual storytelling rather than an enormous paragraph.
4. **Immersion:** At least one meaningful reaction to the visitor (such as pointer, click, touch, or audio interaction).
5. **Mobile friendliness:** The entire portfolio works well on phones and tablets, not just desktop.
6. **Easter egg:** Include at least one hidden, discoverable delight.

The organizers advise keeping each section to roughly **150 words or less** and expressing character through interaction and visuals.

These are official minimums. The extra gameplay and narrative ideas below are **our proposal**, not mandatory hackathon features.

# OUR SELECTED CHARACTER — GLOOB

**Gloob is an original, fictional baby black hole who dreams of becoming a professional space organizer.**

**Core contradiction:** He is deeply committed to putting the universe in order, but the gravity around him accidentally swallows whatever he tries to tidy. This is the *creative engine* of the character and must be visible in the site's behavior—not merely stated in the About copy.

**Project working title:** *Gloob's Cosmic Organization Headquarters*.

**Concept elevator pitch:** Visit the tiny office of Gloob, an earnest baby black hole trying to get his galactic organizing career started. Help him sort stars, reunite misplaced moons with their planets, and arrange orbits while his gravity creates harmless, funny accidents.

**Primary visitor role:** Gloob's temporary assistant, not a passive spectator.

## Gloob's official five-point character sheet (our authored canon)

**1. Name:** Gloob.

**2. What he is:** A tiny, cute baby black hole with a rounded, slightly squishy midnight-purple silhouette, huge bright eyes, a glowing lilac-and-coral accretion ring, little arms, and a small yellow star stuck just out of his reach. He is earnest, ambitious, curious, affectionate, and catastrophically clumsy when handling celestial objects.

**3. What he does:** A self-appointed **Junior Space Organizer**. He helps organize scattered stars, locate lost moons, and create tidy, beautiful orbital arrangements. He wants to earn the title **Certified Galactic Organizer**.

**4. His story:** Born in a cluttered nebula, Gloob decided the universe needed organizing. His first attempt to arrange a constellation ended with the stars disappearing into his tummy. Rather than give up, he opened a tiny organizing office and started learning how to give celestial objects their own proper places—with a little help.

**5. Tagline:** **“A place for everything. Preferably not in my tummy.”**

Keep these five fields visible in the eventual submitted site; don't replace them with trivia, visual badges, or hidden lore.

## Supporting canon and flavor

- **Age:** Three cosmic naps old.
- **Home:** The Little Void, in a fictional neighborhood behind Saturn.
- **Dream:** Become the first Certified Galactic Organizer.
- **Job title:** Junior Space Organizer / Professional Accidental Vacuum (a self-deprecating joke).
- **Biggest fear:** Accidentally swallowing his own to-do list.
- **Greatest weakness:** He sometimes confuses *filing* with *eating*.
- **Temperament:** Well-meaning rather than destructive; proud of tiny successes; embarrassed by mistakes; never malicious.
- **Speech:** Brief, enthusiastic, slightly bureaucratic, with sudden corrections. Example: “That was delicious. I mean—FILED!”
- **Comic pattern:** Gloob proudly announces something is perfectly organized; gravity immediately creates a small new mess; he asks the visitor for help.

**Character guardrails:**
- Cute and recognizable at a small size; avoid intimidating, realistic cosmic-horror visuals.
- Black-hole physics is whimsical; scientific accuracy is not the brief. Do not sell the interactions as astrophysical simulation.
- He must remain sympathetic: mistakes are playful and reversible, and planets with inhabitants are not casually destroyed.
- His professional dream and comic flaw are always more important than random space decorations.
- All artwork must be original or properly credited, and not an established mascot lookalike.

# GLOOB'S WORLD AND CENTRAL STORY

The website is simultaneously a **character portfolio**, a miniature **cosmic office**, and a toy-like **early-web personal homepage**.

Narrative beginning: Gloob forms in a messy nebula and decides that celestial objects ought to have proper homes.

Narrative complication: On his first organizing assignment, he accidentally swallows a constellation and learns that organizing is harder when you're a black hole.

Narrative present: He has built a crooked little space-organization office. Visitors can see examples of his work and help with unfinished jobs.

Narrative ending: The visitor helps him appreciate that organizing the universe is about **giving things room to shine**, not keeping everything close to himself.

**Emotional experience:** Visitors should find Gloob lovable within seconds, giggle at his mistakes, enjoy helping him, and leave with a small sense of accomplishment.

**Storytelling requirement:** Communicate this arc with short copy and visibly changing environments/interactions. Avoid a static wall of lore.

# GLOOB'S THREE PORTFOLIO PROJECTS — CONCEPT ANCHORS

The official rule is to *showcase at least three projects*. Our higher creative goal is to make the three showcases **playable miniature portfolio pieces**. These are the agreed concepts; mechanics, acceptance criteria, and implementation details belong in the later specification.

## 1. Stardust Sorting Co.

**Fictional work:** Gloob's star-cataloging and constellation-tidying service.

**Visitor fantasy:** Help sort a few scattered stars into their appropriate groups while Gloob comments on the process.

**Personality line:** “Tiny stars. Big mess. Very official color-coded filing.”

**Core feeling:** Satisfying organization with cute accidental-gravity mishaps.

## 2. Lost Moon & Found

**Fictional work:** A little celestial lost-property department.

**Visitor fantasy:** Help wandering moons find the planets they belong to, then watch them return to orbit.

**Personality line:** “No moon left behind! Except that one. Wait.”

**Core feeling:** Rescue, recognition, and relief.

## 3. Orbit Organizer 3000

**Fictional work:** A bespoke orbital-layout service for small solar systems.

**Visitor fantasy:** Arrange a miniature system and see celestial objects move along the paths chosen for them.

**Personality line:** “Beautiful orbits, expertly arranged. Please ignore the wobble.”

**Core feeling:** Creative ownership, playful experimentation, and seeing order emerge from a mess.

**Unifying idea:** Each project reveals a different facet of Gloob's job, and together they let the visitor help him get closer to his dream. They should share a recognizable visual language instead of feeling like three unrelated apps.

**Not settled in this file:** Number of objects, placement controls, animation timing, storage, state model, scoring, game difficulty, and exact completion logic. An implementation/specification agent will resolve these within the time limit.

# THE WEBSITE EXPERIENCE — HIGH-LEVEL INTENT

At minimum, the conceptual journey should give visitors:

1. **A memorable first hello:** Immediately meet Gloob, understand his profession, and see him react to the page or visitor.
2. **A tiny origin journey:** Experience the nebula, the first organizational disaster, and his new office.
3. **A portfolio workspace:** Discover and interact with three examples of Gloob's work.
4. **An About/ID card:** Read the mandatory Track 2 five-field character sheet, plus a few funny credentials.
5. **A delightful secret:** Find a concealed side of Gloob's history.
6. **A gentle payoff:** Feel that you've helped Gloob become a slightly better organizer.

**Leading Easter-egg idea:** The yellow star stuck on his head can reveal a secret **Snack Drawer** containing objects he accidentally swallowed (such as stars, a sock, a Wednesday, and his to-do list). This is our concept, not an organizer-prescribed Easter egg; its exact trigger and implementation remain open.

**Leading payoff idea:** Finishing the experiences may award a playful **Temporary Assistant Space Organizer** badge. It is an optional internal reward, not a hackathon prize or organizer credential.

# CREATIVE DIRECTION AND DIFFERENTIATION

**Intended aesthetic:** An eccentric early-2000s personal website colliding with a soft, colorful cosmic toybox: retro windows, sticker-like badges, delightful status messages, starry backgrounds, charmingly odd typography, and a recognizable Gloob avatar.

**Modern usability underneath:** The nostalgia should be in personality and visual detail—not actual 2003 accessibility, performance, or layout problems.

**Design thesis:** Make the character's defining trait—the pull of gravity—the source of memorable interactions, motion, and jokes. A generic space theme with a static black-hole mascot would miss the core opportunity.

**Voice example / possible hero copy (draft, not final):**

> HI!! I'M GLOOB. ✦  
> Junior Space Organizer. Professional Accidental Vacuum.  
> I keep the universe in order. Sort of.  
> Sometimes things fall into my tummy. BUT I'M GETTING BETTER!!

**What makes the idea distinctive:** Instead of showing static fictional case studies, Gloob's portfolio invites the visitor to perform tiny pieces of his improbable profession. His portfolio is a self-contained fictional workplace with an actual character arc.

# OUT OF SCOPE — THINGS THAT WOULD WEAKEN GLOOB

For the initial 48-hour submission, avoid:

- A generic personal-resume template with a Gloob illustration pasted onto it.
- Static project cards with no meaningful visitor participation, if small playable showcases are achievable.
- Complex realistic gravity/N-body simulation just to justify the black-hole premise.
- Heavy mandatory 3D rendering, intricate backend systems, sign-in, or an AI chatbot unless clearly beneficial and everything required already works.
- Long scroll walls of exposition, unreadable novelty fonts, or visual clutter that blocks usability.
- Desktop-only drag interactions with no way to play on touch.
- Auto-playing loud audio or hidden/missing sound controls.
- Fake statistics presented as real visitors or local-only guestbooks described as live.
- Uncredited borrowed assets or imitation of an existing character.
- Scope expansion that risks deployment, mobile QA, or on-time submission.
